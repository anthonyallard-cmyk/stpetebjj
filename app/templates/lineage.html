Historical Roots of Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu

Our BJJ Lineage & Heritage

Tracing the historical martial arts lineage behind Head Instructor Professor Sean Lynch—from Kodokan Judo pioneer Mitsuyo Maeda and the Gracie family in Brazil to the founding of our independent St. Petersburg academy in 2013.

1. Mitsuyo Maeda → 2. Carlos Gracie Sr. → 3. Carlos Gracie Jr. → 4. Eduardo "Veio" de Lima → 5. Professor Sean Lynch (St. Pete BJJ)
Generation 01 • Kodokan Judo & Jiu-Jitsu Pioneer (1878–1941)

Mitsuyo “Count Koma” Maeda

Trained directly at the Kodokan in Tokyo under Judo founder Jigoro Kano, Mitsuyo Maeda traveled the world accepting challenge matches against wrestlers, boxers, and martial artists of every style. Arriving in Belém, Brazil in 1914, Maeda befriended Gastão Gracie and began teaching the art of ground fighting and leverage-based submissions to Gastão’s eldest son, Carlos Gracie.

Generation 02 • Founder of Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu (1902–1994)

Grandmaster Carlos Gracie Sr.

As Maeda’s premier Brazilian student, Carlos Gracie Sr. opened the first Gracie Jiu-Jitsu Academy in Brazil in 1925 alongside his brothers, including Hélio Gracie. Together they refined traditional Japanese techniques into a leverage-and-timing system designed so a smaller defender could overcome a larger, stronger opponent.

Generation 03 • 8th Degree Coral Belt • Founder of IBJJF & Gracie Barra

Master Carlos “Carlinhos” Gracie Jr.

Son of Grandmaster Carlos Gracie Sr., Carlos “Carlinhos” Gracie Jr. dedicated his life to organizing and spreading Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu globally—founding the International Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu Federation (IBJJF), the Confederation of Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu (CBJJ), and the famed Gracie Barra team in Rio de Janeiro in 1986.

Generation 04 • Sean Lynch's Instructor (Retired)

Professor Eduardo “Veio” de Lima

A 6th degree black belt under Carlos “Carlinhos” Gracie Jr., Eduardo “Veio” de Lima began training at age nineteen in Rio de Janeiro at the original Gracie Barra Academy—studying for his first two years under Jean Jacques Machado (blue and purple belt) and thereafter under Carlos Gracie Jr. (brown and black belt, 1997), winning multiple gold medals at the IBJJF Pan American Championships.

After serving as head instructor of Gracie Barra in Rio (where young Roger Gracie was in his kids’ class), Eduardo moved to Florida in 1999/2000. Sean Lynch became one of his earliest American students, earning his black belt from Eduardo in December 2009. (Professor Eduardo is now retired and is not involved in the operation or instruction of St. Pete Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu.)

Generation 05 • 4th Degree Black Belt • Founder & Head Instructor

Professor Sean Lynch — St. Pete Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu

Sean Lynch began training under Eduardo de Lima upon his arrival in the United States in 1999, began teaching in St. Petersburg in 2006, and opened St. Pete Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu as a 100% independent academy in 2013. Awarded his black belt in December 2009 and his 4th degree in December 2023, Sean and our coaching staff carry this lineage forward in an open, ego-free environment.
